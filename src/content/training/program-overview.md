## Program Purpose

The ACE Facilitator Certification Program exists to ensure that every organization receiving an ACE diagnostic gets a consistent, high-quality experience delivered by someone who deeply understands the model, the facilitation methodology, and the organizational dynamics of AI adoption.

Certification matters for three reasons:

**Quality assurance.** The ACE diagnostic is a facilitated self-assessment, not a survey. The quality of the diagnostic depends entirely on the facilitator's ability to create safe space for honest reflection, probe beneath surface-level answers, interpret results accurately, and produce actionable reports. A poorly facilitated diagnostic produces misleading results that lead to misguided investments. Certification ensures facilitators have the skills and knowledge to deliver diagnostics that organizations can trust.

**Brand integrity.** Organizations that engage with ACE are making a decision based on the framework's reputation. Every diagnostic delivered under the ACE name either reinforces or erodes that reputation. Certification creates a quality floor: every certified facilitator has demonstrated competence through supervised practice and assessment, not just self-reported familiarity with the materials.

**Framework fidelity.** ACE is built on specific concepts -- competency as habitual behavior under stress, the progressive competency model, organizational investments over individual training -- that are easy to dilute or misrepresent. Facilitators who understand the model superficially may revert to maturity-model framing, treat the diagnostic as an audit rather than a self-assessment, or recommend zone targets based on aspiration rather than strategic analysis. Certification ensures facilitators internalize the framework's distinctions well enough to maintain them under the pressure of client engagement.

## Program Structure

The certification program is a cohort-based, multi-month training experience designed for working professionals who will continue their regular consulting or engineering work while completing the program.

### Duration

Approximately 3 months from cohort start to certification, depending on scheduling of supervised facilitations.

### Components

The program consists of five sequential training modules, two supervised diagnostic facilitations, and a written assessment:

| Component | Format | Duration |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Module 1: The ACE Model | Intensive or async | 2 days / 8 hours |
| Module 2: Diagnostic Facilitation Skills | Intensive or async | 2 days / 10 hours |
| Module 3: Goal Setting & Zone Selection | Intensive or async | 1 day / 6 hours |
| Module 4: Progression Roadmap Design | Intensive or async | 1 day / 5 hours |
| Module 5a: Facilitator-Led Assessment and Coaching | Intensive or async | 1 day / 5 hours |
| Module 5b: Embedded Delivery and Mentoring | Intensive or async | 1 day / 5 hours |
| Written Assessment | Take-home | 4-6 hours |
| Supervised Facilitation 1 | Live diagnostic | Half-day + debrief |
| Supervised Facilitation 2 | Live diagnostic | Half-day + debrief |

Total instructional time: approximately 39 hours across modules, plus assessment and supervised practice.

### Cohort Model

Trainees participate in cohorts of 4-8 facilitators in training. The cohort model serves several purposes:

- **Peer learning.** Trainees learn from each other's questions, experiences, and perspectives. A cohort that includes consultants, engineering leaders, and coaches produces richer discussion than individual study.
- **Practice partners.** Role-play exercises, mock facilitations, and peer feedback require a group. Individual study cannot replicate the interpersonal dynamics of facilitation.
- **Accountability.** Cohort members support each other's progress and create natural checkpoints for completing work between modules.
- **Community formation.** Cohort relationships become the nucleus of the ongoing facilitator community of practice.

### Module Delivery

Each module can be delivered in two formats:

- **Intensive format.** In-person or synchronous virtual sessions covering the full module content in concentrated time blocks. Best for cohorts that can coordinate schedules.
- **Async format.** Self-paced study of module materials combined with scheduled synchronous sessions for discussion, exercises, and assessment activities. Best for geographically distributed cohorts or cohorts with scheduling constraints.

Both formats include the same learning objectives, activities, and assessments. The async format requires additional self-directed study time to compensate for reduced live interaction.

### Supervised Facilitations

After completing all five modules, each trainee must conduct two complete diagnostic facilitations under the supervision of a master facilitator. These are real diagnostics with real teams, not simulations.

**First supervised facilitation:** The master facilitator observes the entire diagnostic -- pre-workshop preparation, the workshop itself, and report writing. The master facilitator provides detailed feedback on facilitation technique, scoring accuracy, and report quality. If significant issues are identified, the trainee receives targeted coaching before the second facilitation.

**Second supervised facilitation:** The master facilitator observes again, with the expectation that feedback from the first facilitation has been incorporated. The second facilitation serves as the primary practical assessment for certification.

Finding teams for supervised facilitations is the trainee's responsibility, though the program provides guidance on identifying appropriate teams and framing the engagement. Supervised facilitations may be offered at reduced rates or as part of a pilot program to make team recruitment easier.

### Written Assessment

The written assessment is a take-home exam that tests the trainee's ability to apply ACE concepts to realistic scenarios. It covers:

- Model knowledge (zones, proficiencies, investments, the progressive competency model)
- Diagnostic interpretation (given workshop data, determine zone and stage)
- Report writing (given workshop notes, produce team and management reports)
- Goal-setting analysis (given organizational context, recommend target zone with rationale)
- Ethical reasoning (given challenging scenarios, articulate appropriate facilitator behavior)

The minimum passing score is 80%. Trainees who do not pass may retake the assessment once after additional study time.

## Prerequisites

Certification candidates must meet the following prerequisites before joining a cohort:

### Required

- **Experience in software development.** Candidates must have meaningful experience in a software development context -- as an engineer, product manager, designer, engineering manager, QA engineer, or similar role. The specific role matters less than having lived the daily reality of software teams. Facilitators who have never been part of a software team cannot credibly facilitate a diagnostic about software team practices.

- **Familiarity with AI tools in software development.** Candidates should have personal experience using AI tools in a software development context. Ideally, candidates are operating at Zone 2 or above in their own practice -- they use AI tools habitually and understand team-level AI integration from experience, not just theory. Candidates who have only read about AI tools but not used them substantially will struggle to probe team responses with credibility.

- **Experience facilitating team workshops.** Candidates must have prior experience facilitating workshops, retrospectives, or collaborative sessions with teams. The ACE diagnostic is a facilitation exercise, not a presentation or an interview. Candidates need baseline facilitation skills (managing group dynamics, asking open questions, handling disagreement, creating psychological safety) before the program can build ACE-specific facilitation skills on top.

### Recommended

- **Consulting or coaching experience.** Experience working with organizations as an external consultant, internal coach, or change agent. The ACE engagement model involves working with both teams and leadership, navigating organizational politics, and delivering difficult messages diplomatically.

- **Familiarity with organizational competency or maturity frameworks.** Candidates who have experience with diagnostic or maturity frameworks will find many concepts familiar. This is not required but accelerates learning in Module 1.

## Certification Requirements

To achieve certification, a candidate must complete all of the following:

1. **Complete all five training modules** with satisfactory attendance and participation. Each module includes learning activities that require active engagement. Passive attendance is not sufficient.

2. **Pass the written assessment** with a minimum score of 80%. The assessment tests application of knowledge to realistic scenarios, not recall of facts.

3. **Successfully complete two supervised diagnostic facilitations.** Both facilitations must be observed by a master facilitator. The second facilitation serves as the practical certification assessment. "Successfully complete" means the master facilitator confirms that the trainee demonstrated competent facilitation, accurate scoring, appropriate confidentiality management, and acceptable report quality.

4. **Receive sign-off from a master facilitator.** The master facilitator who supervised the facilitations provides a holistic assessment of the candidate's competency to facilitate independently. This sign-off considers the full arc of the candidate's performance across modules, the written assessment, and both supervised facilitations.

### Recertification

Certified facilitators must recertify annually. Recertification verifies continued engagement with the field and continued alignment with the evolving framework. Recertification requires:

- **Continued practice.** Facilitators must have conducted at least two diagnostics in the preceding 12 months. Facilitators who have not practiced risk skill atrophy and loss of currency with the evolving AI landscape.

- **Community of practice participation.** Attendance at a minimum of two quarterly facilitator community calls (see Facilitator Responsibilities below).

- **Framework currency.** Completion of any framework update modules released since the last certification or recertification. As the ACE framework evolves, facilitators must stay current with changes to zone definitions, diagnostic instruments, and facilitation practices.

- **Brief recertification review.** A 30-minute conversation with a master facilitator reviewing recent facilitation experiences, challenges encountered, and any concerns about framework application. This is a supportive check-in, not a high-stakes evaluation.

Facilitators who do not recertify may not conduct diagnostics under the ACE brand until recertification is completed.

## Facilitator Responsibilities

Certified facilitators accept the following responsibilities:

### Diagnostic Quality

- **Only certified facilitators may conduct official ACE diagnostics.** The diagnostic is a branded offering. Running it requires certification, and running it without certification is a violation of the licensing agreement.
- **Facilitators follow the established diagnostic methodology.** This includes the pre-workshop preparation process, the workshop facilitation structure, the scoring methodology, and the dual reporting format. Facilitators may adapt the process to specific organizational contexts but may not omit or substantially alter core elements.
- **Facilitators maintain accurate records of diagnostics conducted.** Records include date, organization (anonymized if required), team size, zone assessment results, and any notable facilitation challenges. These records support recertification and framework improvement.

### Personal Competency

- **Facilitators must maintain their own Zone 2 or higher competency.** A facilitator who does not use AI tools in their own work cannot credibly assess teams that do. Facilitators are expected to be practitioners, not just assessors. They should be able to demonstrate personal experience with the tools, workflows, and practices they are assessing.
- **Facilitators stay current with the evolving AI tool landscape.** AI tools, capabilities, and practices change rapidly. Facilitators need sufficient currency to ask informed follow-up questions, recognize when teams describe outdated practices, and understand the practical implications of the tools teams mention.

### Community Participation

- **Participate in the facilitator community of practice.** The community of practice meets quarterly (virtually or in person) to share experiences, discuss challenging facilitation scenarios, review framework updates, and build collective expertise. Attendance at a minimum of two calls per year is required for recertification.
- **Contribute to collective learning.** Facilitators are encouraged to share anonymized case studies, facilitation techniques, and lessons learned with the community. The community of practice is the primary mechanism for improving facilitation quality across the facilitator network.

### Framework Improvement

- **Report systemic findings to framework stewards.** When facilitators observe patterns across multiple diagnostics -- common failure modes, recurring investment gaps, zones where the diagnostic questions do not capture important distinctions -- they report these observations (anonymized) to the framework stewards. This feedback loop is how the ACE framework improves over time.
- **Participate in framework revision processes.** When the framework stewards propose changes to zone definitions, diagnostic instruments, or facilitation practices, certified facilitators are invited to review and provide feedback. The facilitator community is a key stakeholder in framework evolution.

### Ethical Standards

- **Maintain confidentiality.** Diagnostic results are shared only with the intended recipients (team and management, as defined by the dual reporting structure). Facilitators do not share identifiable diagnostic data with other clients, in marketing materials, or in public forums without explicit written permission.
- **Avoid conflicts of interest.** Facilitators disclose any relationships with the organization being assessed that could compromise objectivity. A facilitator who has a consulting engagement with an organization should not also serve as the diagnostic facilitator for that organization's teams unless the dual role is disclosed and accepted by all parties.
- **Represent the framework accurately.** Facilitators do not misrepresent the ACE framework, its origins, its limitations, or their own qualifications. They do not claim capabilities the framework does not provide (e.g., predictive benchmarking, industry-specific compliance certification).

## Licensing

### What Certification Grants

Certified facilitators receive:

- **License to conduct ACE diagnostics.** Certified facilitators may conduct diagnostics under the ACE brand, using the official diagnostic instruments, facilitation guides, and reporting templates.
- **Access to all framework materials.** This includes diagnostic questionnaires, facilitation guides, scoring rubrics, report templates, roadmap templates, and any other materials developed for the framework. Materials are updated as the framework evolves, and certified facilitators receive updates automatically.
- **Listing in the facilitator registry.** Certified facilitators are listed in the public facilitator registry, which organizations can use to find qualified facilitators. The registry includes the facilitator's name, location, contact information, and certification date.
- **Use of the ACE facilitator designation.** Certified facilitators may identify themselves as "Certified ACE Facilitator" in professional materials, proposals, and marketing.

### Licensing Terms

- The facilitator license is annual, aligned with the recertification cycle.
- The license is personal and non-transferable. Certified facilitators may not delegate diagnostic facilitation to uncertified individuals.
- The license grants the right to use ACE materials and branding for diagnostic engagements. It does not grant ownership of the materials or the right to modify and redistribute them.
- Facilitators who allow their certification to lapse must complete recertification before resuming diagnostic work under the ACE brand.

### Licensing Fees

Licensing fees are set by the framework stewards and published separately. Fees support framework development, community infrastructure, and program administration. Fee structures are designed to be accessible to independent consultants as well as larger consulting firms.

## Program Modules

The five training modules build on each other sequentially. Each module has detailed learning objectives, content outlines, learning activities, and assessments documented in separate module guides:

1. **[Module 1: The ACE Model](/training/module-1-model-foundations/)** -- Deep understanding of the framework: zones, competency, progressive competency, organizational investments. The knowledge foundation on which all facilitation skills are built.

2. **[Module 2: Diagnostic Facilitation Skills](/training/module-2-diagnostic-facilitation/)** -- How to prepare for, run, and report on a complete ACE diagnostic workshop. The core facilitation competency.

3. **[Module 3: Goal Setting & Zone Selection](/training/module-3-goal-setting/)** -- How to facilitate zone-selection sessions with organizational leadership. Connecting diagnostic results to strategic decisions.

4. **[Module 4: Progression Roadmap Design](/training/module-4-roadmap-design/)** -- How to design realistic, zone-specific progression roadmaps with appropriate leading indicators and reassessment cadences.

5. **Module 5a: [Facilitator-Led Assessment and Coaching](/training/module-5-coaching-engagement/)** -- How to support organizations through zone transitions as the ACE Facilitator: structured check-ins, investment retrospectives, re-diagnostics, regression handling, and managing the ongoing facilitator-client relationship within engagements that include embedded Artisan teams.

6. **Module 5b: [Embedded Delivery and Mentoring](/training/module-5b-embedded-delivery/)** -- How to deliver software alongside client teams as an embedded Artisan: mentoring through collaborative work, maintaining the capability-building purpose, cross-craft collaboration, ramp-down planning, and relationship management as an embedded team member. (For Artisans; Facilitator certification not required.)
