This document defines the four-phase consulting engagement structure for ACE diagnostic and advisory work. It is designed to be specific enough to quote in a proposal and flexible enough to adapt to organizations of different sizes, industries, and AI adoption stages.

---

## Engagement Overview

An ACE engagement guides an organization from "we don't know where we are" to "we have a roadmap and are making measurable progress." The engagement follows four phases:

1. **Discovery** -- Understand the organization's context before any assessment begins.
2. **Diagnostic** -- Facilitate team self-assessments to establish current zones and competency stages.
3. **Goal Setting & Roadmap** -- Help leadership choose target zones and build progression plans.
4. **Implementation Support** -- Ongoing advisory to track progress, adjust course, and re-assess.

Each phase produces specific deliverables. Phases are sequential: Discovery informs the Diagnostic, the Diagnostic informs Goal Setting, and the Roadmap guides Implementation Support.

---

## Phase 1: Discovery

**Duration:** 2-4 weeks

**Purpose:** Build a thorough understanding of the organization's context -- size, development maturity, current AI usage, competitive position, stakeholder perspectives, and potential constraints -- before administering any diagnostic. The Discovery phase prevents the common failure mode of running an assessment without understanding the environment, which produces technically accurate results that miss organizational reality.

### Activities

**Stakeholder Interviews (1-2 weeks)**

Conduct structured interviews with three stakeholder tiers:

| Stakeholder Tier | Typical Roles | Interview Duration | Focus Areas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business Leadership | CEO, COO, Product VP, Business Unit Leads | 45-60 minutes each | Strategic goals, competitive pressure, investment appetite, risk tolerance |
| Engineering Leadership | CTO, VP Engineering, Engineering Directors, Engineering Managers | 60-90 minutes each | Technical infrastructure, team structure, development practices, AI tool status |
| Individual Contributors | Senior developers, tech leads, PMs, designers | 45-60 minutes each (sample of 3-5 per team) | Daily workflow, AI tool usage, pain points, team dynamics |

See [Stakeholder Interview Guide](/toolkit/stakeholder-interview-guide) for question sets.

**Context Analysis (1-2 weeks)**

Compile findings into a structured context analysis covering:

- Organization profile (size, industry, team composition, geographic distribution)
- Current AI tool inventory (what is deployed, who uses it, how consistently)
- Development maturity baseline (CI/CD practices, testing culture, agile practices, deployment frequency)
- AI-relevant organizational context (security policies, compliance requirements, procurement process, data sensitivity)
- Competitive landscape (what are peers and competitors doing with AI-augmented development?)
- Key stakeholders and their perspectives on AI adoption
- Initial zone hypothesis (the facilitator's pre-diagnostic estimate of where teams likely fall)

See [Context Analysis Template](/toolkit/context-analysis-template) for the full template.

**Pre-Diagnostic Brief (half-day session)**

Before the Diagnostic phase begins, conduct a briefing session with engineering leadership and team leads:

- Explain the ACE model, the four zones, and what the diagnostic measures
- Clarify that this is a facilitated self-assessment, not an external audit or performance evaluation
- Set expectations about what the diagnostic will and will not reveal
- Address concerns about how results will be used
- Confirm logistics for the Diagnostic phase (team schedules, workshop rooms, participant lists)

This session is essential for preventing defensive reactions during the diagnostic. When teams understand that the assessment exists to help them identify investments, not to judge their performance, the quality of self-assessment improves dramatically.

### Deliverables

| Deliverable | Description | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| Context Analysis Report | Comprehensive organizational context document | Facilitator internal use; summary shared with engagement sponsor |
| Stakeholder Summary | Key themes, concerns, and alignment points from interviews | Engagement sponsor and engineering leadership |
| Pre-Diagnostic Brief Deck | Materials used in the briefing session | Engineering leadership, team leads |
| Initial Zone Hypothesis | Facilitator's pre-diagnostic estimate per team | Facilitator internal use only (not shared with client to avoid anchoring) |

### Client Involvement Required

- **Engagement Sponsor:** 2-3 hours total (kickoff meeting, context review, pre-diagnostic brief)
- **Business Leadership:** 45-60 minutes each for interviews (typically 2-4 leaders)
- **Engineering Leadership:** 60-90 minutes each for interviews, plus attendance at pre-diagnostic brief (typically 3-6 leaders)
- **Individual Contributors:** 45-60 minutes each for interviews (3-5 per team being assessed)
- **Logistics Coordinator:** Someone from the client side to schedule interviews and reserve workshop space

## Phase 2: Diagnostic

**Duration:** 1-2 weeks (depends on number of teams)

**Purpose:** Administer the ACE diagnostic to each team through facilitated workshops. Produce individual team reports and a consolidated management report with systemic findings.

### Activities

**Team Diagnostic Workshops (half-day per team)**

Each team participates in a facilitated self-assessment workshop. The facilitator guides the team through the zone-specific diagnostic questions, facilitates discussion about evidence and examples, and helps the team reach honest self-assessment of their current zone and competency stage.

Workshop structure:
- **Opening (15 min):** Review the ACE model, explain the workshop process, emphasize that this is self-assessment not evaluation
- **Baseline Screening (15 min):** Confirm the team is past Zone 0 using the baseline screening questions
- **Zone 1 Assessment (45-60 min):** Work through Zone 1 diagnostic questions, discuss evidence, reach consensus on competency stage
- **Zone 2 Assessment (45-60 min):** If Zone 1 score indicates competency, assess Zone 2 proficiencies
- **Zone 3 Assessment (30-45 min):** If applicable, assess Zone 3 proficiencies (many teams will not reach this)
- **Zone 4 Assessment (30-45 min):** If applicable, assess Zone 4 proficiencies (requires Zone 3 competency)
- **Closing (15 min):** Summarize findings, explain next steps, address questions

Scheduling guidance:
- Assess no more than 2 teams per day (facilitator fatigue degrades quality)
- Allow 30-60 minutes between workshops for facilitator notes
- Schedule workshops in a private room where the team can speak candidly

**Score Aggregation and Analysis (2-3 days)**

After all workshops are complete:
- Calculate zone scores and competency stages for each team
- Identify patterns across teams (systemic strengths, systemic gaps)
- Compare findings against the initial zone hypothesis from Discovery
- Identify organizational-level factors that affect multiple teams
- Draft team reports and management report

**Team Report Generation (1-2 days)**

Produce individual reports for each team. Each team report includes:
- Current zone and competency stage with evidence
- Strengths (proficiencies where the team scored highest)
- Growth areas (proficiencies where the team scored lowest)
- Comparison to organizational average (where relevant)
- Specific investment recommendations for the team's next stage

**Management Report Generation (2-3 days)**

Produce a consolidated report for leadership. The management report includes:
- Executive summary of findings across all teams
- Zone distribution visualization (how many teams at each zone/stage)
- Systemic findings (organizational factors affecting multiple teams)
- Investment themes (common investments that would benefit multiple teams)
- Comparison to industry benchmarks (where available)
- Recommended target zones with rationale

### Deliverables

| Deliverable | Description | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| Team Diagnostic Reports | Individual report per team with zone, stage, strengths, gaps, and recommendations | Team leads, engineering managers, team members |
| Management Report | Consolidated findings, systemic analysis, and strategic recommendations | CTO, VP Engineering, engagement sponsor, executive leadership |
| Raw Score Data | Anonymized scoring data for reference | Facilitator internal use; available to client on request |

### Client Involvement Required

- **Full team participation:** Each team member attends their team's half-day workshop (typically 4-8 people per team)
- **Engineering Managers:** Available for clarifying questions during analysis (30 minutes as needed)
- **No leadership presence in workshops:** Team workshops should be attended by the team only. Leadership presence changes self-assessment dynamics and produces less honest results.

### Facilitator Capacity

- One facilitator can assess 2 teams per day, maximum 8-10 teams per week
- For engagements with more than 10 teams, plan for either multiple facilitators or extend the Diagnostic phase across multiple weeks
- Report generation requires 1-2 days of dedicated facilitator time after workshops conclude

## Phase 3: Goal Setting & Roadmap

**Duration:** 1-2 weeks

**Purpose:** Translate diagnostic findings into strategic decisions (target zones) and tactical plans (progression roadmaps). This phase bridges assessment and action.

### Activities

**Leadership Goal-Setting Session (half-day)**

A facilitated session with engineering leadership and business leadership to:
- Present the Management Report findings
- Discuss systemic patterns and organizational factors
- Review the decision framework for zone selection (see [Goal-Setting Framework](/toolkit/goal-setting-framework))
- Evaluate risk appetite, investment capacity, and strategic need
- Declare target zones for each team or workstream
- Identify organizational-level investments required

This session should include decision-makers who can commit resources. Without budget authority and organizational authority in the room, target zone declarations become aspirational rather than actionable.

**Team Roadmap Sessions (2-3 hours per team)**

For each team, the facilitator conducts a roadmap-building session:
- Review the team's diagnostic report together
- Present the leadership-declared target zone for the team
- Identify the specific investments required for the team's zone transition
- Build a phased roadmap with milestones, activities, and success criteria
- Identify dependencies on organizational-level investments
- Establish leading indicators that will signal progress or stalls
- Set the first re-diagnostic date

See the roadmap templates for zone-specific progression plans:
- [Baseline to Zone 1](/toolkit/baseline-to-zone-1)
- [Zone 1 to Zone 2](/toolkit/zone-1-to-2)
- [Zone 2 to Zone 3](/toolkit/zone-2-to-3)
- [Zone 3 to Zone 4](/toolkit/zone-3-to-4)

### Deliverables

| Deliverable | Description | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| Target Zone Declaration | Formal document stating each team's current zone, target zone, and strategic rationale | All stakeholders |
| Progression Roadmaps | Phased plan per team with milestones, activities, success criteria, and timeline | Team leads, engineering managers, teams |
| Organizational Investment Plan | Cross-team investments required (tooling, policy, training, structural changes) | CTO, VP Engineering, budget owners |
| Leading Indicators Dashboard | Metrics to track for early signals of progress or stalls | Engineering leadership, facilitator |

### Client Involvement Required

- **Business Leadership:** Half-day goal-setting session (CEO/COO optional but valuable; CTO/VP Eng required)
- **Engineering Leadership:** Half-day goal-setting session plus attendance at their team's roadmap session
- **Team Leads:** 2-3 hours for their team's roadmap session
- **Budget Authority:** Someone who can approve investment commitments during or shortly after the goal-setting session

## Phase 4: Implementation Support

**Duration:** Ongoing, typically 3-12 months

**Purpose:** Provide ongoing advisory support as the organization executes its roadmap. This phase prevents the common failure mode of producing a great plan that sits in a drawer. Implementation Support keeps the organization accountable, helps navigate obstacles, and adjusts the roadmap as conditions change.

### Activities

**Periodic Check-ins (monthly or bi-monthly)**

Regular sessions with engineering leadership and team leads to:
- Review progress against roadmap milestones
- Discuss leading indicators and what they signal
- Identify blockers and develop mitigation strategies
- Share cross-team learnings (what is working for one team may help another)
- Adjust timelines and activities as needed

Check-in format:
- 60-90 minutes with engineering leadership (monthly)
- 30-45 minutes with individual team leads (bi-monthly or as needed)

**Metrics Tracking Setup**

At the start of Implementation Support, work with the engineering leadership team to establish a shared metrics framework:

- Review the [Metrics Tree](/toolkit/metrics-tree) to identify the North Star metric and zone-specific leading indicators relevant to the team's target zone
- Pull the applicable zone-specific metrics from [Zone-Specific Metrics](/toolkit/zone-specific-metrics) — these are the behavioral measures most predictive of competency progression
- Add [Organizational Health Metrics](/toolkit/organizational-health-metrics) where the organization shows early warning signs in culture, safety, or capacity

The metrics framework becomes the standing agenda for monthly check-ins: leading indicators are reviewed at each check-in, and any indicator that has moved in an unexpected direction becomes a discussion topic. Do not wait for re-diagnostics to notice stalls — the purpose of leading indicators is to surface problems while they are still correctable.

**Facilitated Investment Retrospectives (quarterly)**

Structured retrospective sessions that examine:
- What investments are producing results?
- What investments are stalling and why?
- Are organizational-level investments keeping pace with team-level progress?
- Do any teams need their target zone reconsidered?
- What new obstacles or opportunities have emerged?

**Re-Diagnostic (at 3-6 month intervals)**

Administer the diagnostic again to measure actual competency progression:
- Same facilitated workshop format as the initial diagnostic
- Compare results to baseline scores
- Identify areas of genuine progress and areas of stagnation
- Produce updated team reports and management report
- Adjust roadmaps based on re-diagnostic findings

Re-diagnostics are essential because competency is behavioral, not declarative. Teams may believe they have progressed when their habits under pressure have not actually changed. The re-diagnostic provides an honest check.

**Roadmap Refresh (following each re-diagnostic)**

Update the progression roadmap based on re-diagnostic findings:
- Advance milestones that have been achieved
- Extend timelines where progress is slower than planned
- Add new activities to address newly identified gaps
- Remove activities that proved unnecessary
- Update leading indicators if current ones are not predictive

### Deliverables

| Deliverable | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Progress Report | Summary of roadmap progress, leading indicators, and blockers | Monthly |
| Investment Retrospective Report | Findings from quarterly retrospective | Quarterly |
| Re-Diagnostic Reports | Updated team and management reports | Every 3-6 months |
| Updated Roadmap | Refreshed progression plan reflecting current state | After each re-diagnostic |

### Client Involvement Required

- **Engineering Leadership:** 60-90 minutes monthly for check-ins, half-day quarterly for investment retrospectives
- **Team Leads:** 30-45 minutes bi-monthly for check-ins, participation in re-diagnostic workshops
- **Full Teams:** Half-day per re-diagnostic workshop (same as initial diagnostic)
- **Engagement Sponsor:** Quarterly review of progress and investment decisions

## Typical Engagement Timeline

| Phase | Duration | Teams = 3 | Teams = 8 | Teams = 15+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | 2-4 weeks | 2 weeks | 3 weeks | 4 weeks |
| Diagnostic | 1-2 weeks | 1 week | 1.5 weeks | 2-3 weeks (or multiple facilitators) |
| Goal Setting & Roadmap | 1-2 weeks | 1 week | 1.5 weeks | 2 weeks |
| Implementation Support | 3-12 months | 3-6 months typical | 6-12 months typical | 6-12 months typical |
| **Total initial engagement** | **4-8 weeks + ongoing** | **4-5 weeks + ongoing** | **6-7 weeks + ongoing** | **8-9 weeks + ongoing** |

The initial engagement (Phases 1-3) is typically scoped as a fixed-price engagement. Implementation Support (Phase 4) is structured as a retainer or time-and-materials arrangement.

## Pricing Model Guidance

The following pricing structures are appropriate for ACE engagements. Actual rates depend on market, facilitator experience, and organizational complexity.

### Option A: Phased Fixed-Price

| Phase | Scope | Suggested Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Fixed scope, fixed deliverables | Fixed price per engagement |
| Diagnostic | Per-team pricing | Base price + per-team increment |
| Goal Setting & Roadmap | Fixed scope following diagnostic | Fixed price per engagement |
| Implementation Support | Ongoing | Monthly retainer or time-and-materials |

This model provides cost predictability for the client and clear scope boundaries for the facilitator.

### Option B: Full Initial Engagement Package

Bundle Phases 1-3 into a single fixed-price engagement, priced by organization size:

| Organization Size | Teams Assessed | Typical Range |
|---|---|---|
| Small (1-3 teams) | 1-3 | Lower tier |
| Medium (4-8 teams) | 4-8 | Mid tier |
| Large (9-15+ teams) | 9-15+ | Upper tier (may require multiple facilitators) |

Implementation Support is always priced separately as an ongoing retainer.

### Option C: Annual Advisory Relationship

For organizations that want ongoing support, structure the engagement as an annual advisory:

- Initial diagnostic and roadmap (Phases 1-3) included in Year 1
- Quarterly re-diagnostics included
- Monthly check-ins and advisory included
- Annual renewal based on continued need

This model works well for larger organizations with multiple teams progressing at different rates.

### Pricing Principles

- **Never price by zone.** Pricing should reflect facilitator effort, not the client's current or target zone. A Zone 0-to-1 engagement requires the same diagnostic rigor as a Zone 2-to-3 engagement.
- **Re-diagnostics are separate deliverables.** Each re-diagnostic requires workshop facilitation and report generation. Price accordingly.
- **Travel and expenses are additional.** For on-site engagements, travel costs are passed through at cost.
- **Multi-facilitator engagements carry coordination overhead.** When multiple facilitators are needed, add coordination time to the scope.

## Facilitator Requirements

### For a Solo Facilitator

- Maximum of 8-10 teams per engagement (without extending the diagnostic phase beyond 2 weeks)
- Maximum of 2 diagnostic workshops per day
- Require 2-3 dedicated days for report generation after workshops conclude
- Plan for 1-2 days between phases for preparation

### For Multi-Facilitator Engagements

- Designate a lead facilitator responsible for the Management Report and cross-team analysis
- All facilitators must be calibrated on scoring methodology before the diagnostic begins
- Schedule a calibration session (half-day) before the diagnostic phase starts
- The lead facilitator conducts the leadership goal-setting session; other facilitators attend their teams' roadmap sessions

### Facilitator Competencies

An ACE facilitator should have:

- Direct experience with AI-augmented software development (personal competency at Zone 2 or above)
- Facilitation experience with team retrospectives, workshops, or organizational assessments
- Familiarity with organizational competency or maturity diagnostic frameworks
- Ability to synthesize qualitative data from interviews and workshops into actionable reports
- Comfort with organizational dynamics (navigating political sensitivities, managing defensive reactions, delivering difficult findings)

## Engagement Anti-Patterns

The following patterns reduce the effectiveness of an ACE engagement:

- **Skipping Discovery.** Running the diagnostic without understanding organizational context produces results that are technically accurate but strategically useless. Discovery is not optional.
- **Leadership in the room during diagnostic workshops.** When managers attend their team's self-assessment, team members self-censor. Results skew optimistic and the diagnostic loses its value.
- **Treating the diagnostic as a performance review.** If teams believe their scores will affect performance evaluations, they will game the assessment. The facilitator must establish -- and leadership must reinforce -- that the diagnostic exists to identify investments, not to judge individuals.
- **Setting target zones without investment commitment.** Declaring a target zone without committing the organizational investments required to reach it is aspirational theater. Phase 3 must include real investment decisions.
- **Skipping re-diagnostics.** Without periodic re-assessment, organizations lose accountability. The roadmap becomes a historical document rather than a living plan. Re-diagnostics are not optional extras; they are how the engagement produces lasting value.
- **Rushing from diagnostic to "doing things."** The goal-setting and roadmap phase exists to ensure that action is directed by strategy, not by urgency. Skipping structured roadmapping in favor of "just start improving" produces scattered effort and minimal competency gains.
