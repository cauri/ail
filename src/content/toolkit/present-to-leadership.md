This guide helps facilitators prepare a client-side presenter (typically an engineering leader or change agent) to bring ACE diagnostic results and a progression plan to their leadership. The goal is to frame zone transitions as business investments that leadership can evaluate, fund, and champion -- not as "AI training" requests that get deprioritized.

## Prerequisites

Before the presentation, the facilitator should ensure the client presenter has:

- Completed ACE diagnostic results for one or more teams
- A recommended target zone with supporting rationale. See [How to Choose a Target Zone](/toolkit/choose-target-zone).
- A draft progression roadmap. See [How to Create a Progression Roadmap](/toolkit/create-progression-roadmap).

## What to Include in the Management Report

The management report should focus on systemic patterns, not individual team performance. Leadership needs to understand organizational capability, not grade individual teams.

### Current State Summary

Present the diagnostic findings as an organizational portrait:

- **Distribution of teams across zones.** How many teams are at each zone? What is the range?
- **Common competency gaps.** Which proficiencies are weakest across teams? These indicate systemic issues rather than individual team problems.
- **Organizational blockers.** What systemic friction is preventing progress? Policy restrictions, procurement delays, infrastructure limitations, management practices.

Avoid naming specific teams or individuals in the report. The goal is to identify patterns that require organizational action, not to create a leaderboard.

**Small organization caution.** In organizations with fewer than 5 teams, the 'distribution of teams across zones' may effectively identify specific teams. Consider whether organizational-level aggregation (without team-level distribution) is more appropriate. The principle: anonymization must be real, not nominal. If any reader of the report could reasonably identify a specific team from the reported data, the reporting structure should be adjusted. Options include: reporting only the organizational zone range (e.g., 'teams range from Zone 1 Developing to Zone 2 Emerging') without a team count at each level, or presenting the management report in a joint meeting where teams are present.

### The Investment Framing

Frame every recommendation as a business investment with identifiable costs and returns. Leadership evaluates investments, not aspirations.

**Instead of:** "We need to invest in AI training for our teams."

**Say:** "Our diagnostic shows that 70% of our teams are at Zone 1. Progressing to Zone 2 requires organizational investments in [specific items]. Based on industry benchmarks and our diagnostic data, we can expect [specific benefits] within [timeline]. The total investment is [amount] over [period]."

For each recommended investment, provide:

- **What it costs.** Be specific about budget, time, and organizational attention required.
- **What it produces.** Tie benefits to metrics leadership cares about: delivery speed, quality, team retention, competitive position.
- **When returns appear.** Set realistic expectations. Zone 1 benefits appear in weeks. Zone 2 benefits take months. Zones 3-4 operate on 12-24 month timelines.
- **What happens if we don't invest.** Articulate the cost of inaction in terms of competitive disadvantage, talent attrition, or unrealized productivity.

### Zone-Specific ROI Narratives

Tailor the ROI narrative to the specific zone transition being proposed. The examples below are written in the voice of the client presenter -- language they can adapt for their own leadership audience.

**Zone 0 to 1:** "Our developers are not yet using AI tools habitually. This means we are paying for slower individual task completion, longer onboarding times, and more time spent on boilerplate work than our competitors. The investment to reach Zone 1 is modest -- primarily tool licenses, training time, and policy clarity -- and returns appear within 2-4 weeks of adoption."

**Zone 1 to 2:** "Our developers use AI tools individually, but our team processes have not adapted. We are capturing individual productivity gains but missing the systematic improvements that come from integrating AI into delivery workflows. The investment includes infrastructure changes, process updates, and dedicated team time. Returns include reduced delivery variability, systematic quality improvements, and faster cycle times."

**Zone 2 to 3:** "We have integrated AI into our team workflows, but our engineering model is still fundamentally traditional. Zone 3 transforms the engineering role itself, enabling significantly higher leverage per engineer. This requires restructuring roles, compensation, and team topology. It is a larger investment with a longer payoff horizon, appropriate for organizations where engineering leverage is a competitive differentiator."

**Zone 3 to 4:** "We want AI to be a strategic organizational capability, not just an engineering tool. This requires executive-level commitment, dedicated capital allocation, and specialized talent acquisition. The returns are potentially transformative but operate on multi-year timelines with genuine uncertainty."

## How to Present the Roadmap

Present the roadmap as a phased investment plan, not a project plan. Leadership responds to investment logic: "We invest X to get Y by Z" rather than "here is a list of things we need to do."

Key presentation elements:

- **Phases with clear decision points.** Each phase should end with a reassessment that informs whether to continue, adjust, or pause.
- **Leading indicators.** Show leadership how they will know the investment is working before the final assessment.
- **Explicit off-ramps.** Make clear that the roadmap includes checkpoints where the organization evaluates progress and decides on next steps -- which may include continuing to the next phase, consolidating at the current zone, adjusting the timeline, or redirecting investment. The decision is genuinely open; the facilitator presents data and options. This reduces the perceived risk of commitment.
- **Human cost transparency.** For Zone 3 and Zone 4 transitions, be direct about the human dimension: role identity changes, potential workforce displacement concerns, and the emotional cost of letting go of craft identity. Leadership that understands the human cost makes better investment decisions than leadership that sees only the financial and operational dimensions.
- **Resource requirements by phase.** Avoid asking for 12 months of commitment upfront. Ask for Phase 1 commitment with a decision point before Phase 2.

## Common Leadership Objections and Responses

The responses below are written in the voice of the client presenter. Coach the presenter to adapt these to their organization's context and terminology.

### "We already bought AI tool licenses. Why isn't that enough?"

Tool access is necessary but not sufficient. The diagnostic shows that our teams have the tools but lack the organizational support -- clear policies, integrated workflows, management endorsement -- to use them habitually. The tool licenses are the foundation; the remaining investments unlock their value.

### "Can't we just let teams figure this out on their own?"

Some teams will. Most will not. The diagnostic shows that without organizational investment, AI adoption is inconsistent and fragile -- it drops off under pressure. Organizational investment creates the conditions for sustainable, reliable adoption across all teams rather than pockets of excellence.

### "This sounds expensive. What's the minimum viable investment?"

Zone 1 requires modest investment: policy clarity, training time, and management endorsement alongside the tool licenses you already have. Zone 2 requires infrastructure and process changes. Focus on Zone 1-to-2 progression as the minimum -- it produces the strongest return relative to investment. Zones 3 and 4 require larger investments that should be justified by strategic analysis.

### "How do we know this will work?"

The ACE framework includes built-in reassessment. We propose a phased approach with diagnostic checkpoints at each phase boundary. If the leading indicators are not showing progress, we adjust the roadmap before committing to the next phase. You are never more than one phase away from a decision point.

### "Our teams are already using AI. We're fine."

The diagnostic distinguishes between usage and competency. Usage means teams use AI tools when conditions are favorable. Competency means they use them habitually, even under deadline pressure and in unfamiliar contexts. The diagnostic results show where that gap exists in your organization.

### "We need to move faster. Can we skip zones?"

Zones build on each other. Teams cannot systematize AI use (Zone 2) without individual AI competency (Zone 1). Organizations cannot transform engineering roles (Zone 3) without team-level AI integration (Zone 2). However, you can accelerate progression through more concentrated investment. The roadmap phases are typical timelines, not minimum timelines.

## Securing Ongoing Commitment

A single presentation is not enough. Leadership commitment must be sustained across the roadmap timeline. Build in:

- **Regular progress updates.** Brief, data-driven updates tied to leading indicators. Monthly or quarterly, depending on the pace of change.
- **Reassessment presentations.** When each diagnostic reassessment is complete, present the results to leadership. Show movement, celebrate progress, and surface new blockers that need organizational action.
- **Success stories.** Identify and share concrete examples of how AI competency is producing business value. Specific stories are more compelling than aggregate metrics.
- **Visible executive sponsorship.** Identify an executive sponsor who is willing to champion the initiative publicly. Their visible support signals organizational commitment and reduces the risk that teams will treat the initiative as optional.

## Report Structure Template

```
ACE Diagnostic Report: [Organization Name]
=================================================

Executive Summary
- Current state: [zone distribution across teams]
- Key finding: [most important systemic insight]
- Recommendation: [target zone and headline investment]

Current State Assessment
- Zone distribution: [summary table or chart]
- Common competency gaps: [top 3-5 gaps across teams]
- Systemic blockers: [organizational friction points]

Recommended Target Zone
- Target: Zone [X]
- Rationale: [strategic justification, 2-3 sentences]
- Alternative considered: [why a different zone was not chosen]

Investment Plan
- Phase 1: [summary, cost, timeline, expected outcome]
- Phase 2: [summary, cost, timeline, expected outcome]
- Decision point: [what we assess before committing to Phase 3]

Expected Returns
- Short-term (1-3 months): [specific benefits]
- Medium-term (3-6 months): [specific benefits]
- Long-term (6-12 months): [specific benefits]

Reassessment Schedule
- [dates and what each reassessment will measure]
```
